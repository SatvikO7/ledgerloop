# PITCH.md — the five-minute walkthrough

A run-sheet for the demo video and for a judge with five minutes. Every figure
below is quoted from [EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md), which is generated by
`make eval` and never hand-typed. Setup and troubleshooting live in
[DEMO.md](DEMO.md); the reasoning lives in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md).


## 0:00–0:40 · The problem

Three systems describe the same money and none of them agrees: the **ledger**
knows what was ordered, the **PSP settlement report** knows what was batched and
what fees were taken, the **bank statement** knows what actually arrived.

The hard part is not string matching. **Many payments become one payout.** A
settlement nets dozens of payments minus a fee, and the bank shows a single
credit with a narration like:

```
NEFT-UTR2026030946866-ZRDHA BRKNG L-MERCHANT PAYOUT
```

Recovering which payments are inside that credit is a subset-sum problem — and
in finance ops **a wrong auto-match costs far more than a human reviewing an
extra item.** That asymmetry is the whole design.

> Say the number early: **0 false positives, ₹0 of wrong money.**

## 0:40–1:10 · The inputs

Show the three files in `data/generated/test-standard-42/`. Point out they are
deliberately heterogeneous — CSV, JSON, CSV; `DD/MM/YYYY` in the bank file;
amounts in paise as integers everywhere; ~20% of PSP order references corrupted
(null, space-separated, or carrying a non-breaking hyphen).

**Say it plainly: this data is synthetic.** Eleven anomaly classes at controlled
prevalence from a seeded generator, with link-level ground truth emitted from the
generator's own record of what it did — never inferred back out of the files.

## 1:10–2:10 · The ladder, on the Pipeline screen

Six rungs, each seeing only what the previous ones left:

| Tier | Decides on | Proposed → auto-matched |
|---|---|---|
| **T0** exact key | UTR / order reference, exact amount | 395 → 395 |
| **T1** tolerance | ±0.5% or ₹1, ±3 days, **mutual** uniqueness | 49 → 49 |
| **T2** aggregation | meet-in-the-middle subset-sum | 86 → 85 |
| **T3** lexical | merchant master **derived from the statement's own references** | 70 → 70 |
| **T4** graph | path closure, sibling completion, exclusivity | **0 → 0** |
| **T5** LLM | proposes only | did not run |

Two of those rows are the ones to dwell on.

**T2 counts solutions and refuses when two fit.** One settlement per corpus is
genuinely ambiguous — two payment subsets reach the same tranche within
tolerance — and the system refuses it rather than picking. A test pins that
refusal so it cannot be optimised away.

**T4 shows 0 and the panel says why.** Every earlier rung matches at settlement
granularity, so the partial assignments graph inference exists to finish never
arise. It is measured on all 29 corpora and again at 5,000 orders: zero partial
states in either. **Do not claim it contributes.** Correct, unexercised, and
pinned by an invariant test — and not dead weight: on this corpus its exclusivity
pass fully absorbs 27 credits, work later rungs would otherwise have to redo.

## 2:10–2:50 · Evidence, and refusal

Open the **Evidence** screen and follow one record end to end: source records →
normalisation → candidate → tier → arithmetic verification → decision.

Then open **Exceptions**, sorted by rupee impact, and open the largest one.
Every item carries a class, a severity, a **rupee figure**, an evidence chain
pointing back at source records, and a suggested action.

The shape of sentence the project exists to produce — read the real one off the
screen, this is the archetype, not a record to quote:

> *"₹4,312 short because a chargeback was netted off SETL-0091."*

**Refusal is a feature, not a gap.** 67 exceptions covering ₹50,20,195.68, of
which 35 are `UNMATCHABLE` — irreconcilable from the three sources by
construction. They are excluded from the match-rate denominator and reported on
their own line, because a system that counted them inside its recall could
inflate the number by describing items nobody could resolve.

## 2:50–3:40 · Evaluation — and why the verdicts are conservative

All four systems scored against the same ground truth by the same `evaluate()`
call:

| | Precision | Recall | Match rate | FP | FP cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B0 exact join | 59.09% | 66.33% | 65.80% | 135 | ₹34,98,306 |
| B1 + fuzzy + nearest amount | 65.40% | 88.10% | 86.67% | 137 | ₹35,37,867 |
| B2 LLM-only (`dev`) | 100.00% | 18.64% | 17.65% | 0 | ₹0 |
| **B3 LedgerLoop T0–T4** | **100.00%** | **96.26%** | **91.59%** | **0** | **₹0** |

The deterministic ladder beats every baseline on recall *and* is the only one
never wrong. **The false-positive column is what the others pay for the
difference.**

Now show the verdict column, because it is the most defensible thing in the
document:

| Metric | Value (95% Wilson CI, n) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Auto-match precision | 1.0000 [0.9866, 1.0000] (n=283) | **undecided** — the interval straddles ≥ 99% |
| Match rate | 0.9159 [0.8819, 0.9408] (n=345) | **met** |
| Exception recall | 1.0000 [0.8865, 1.0000] (n=30) | **undecided** — 30 of 30 is not ≥ 95% from thirty records |

> **This is the line to say out loud:** two of our three headline metrics are
> perfect scores that we refuse to call a pass, because the sample is too small
> to support it. The verdict reads the interval, not the point estimate.

Across five seeds: precision 1.0000 ± 0.0000, recall 0.8844 ± 0.0788, match rate
0.8533 ± 0.0810, exception recall 0.9818 ± 0.0250.

## 3:40–4:10 · The LLM layer, and the control that proves it

The model may read a narration the regex layer could not, propose a link for the
residual, and rewrite exception prose. It may **never** decide a match, do
arithmetic, set a probability, produce a metric, or classify an exception.

Measured **live against Gemini** (`gemini-3.6-flash`): 9 calls, 13,097 tokens,
₹8.81 equivalent-paid cost. Six of fifteen calls failed — three timeouts, three
503s — and the ladder absorbed all six. **Nine outputs were refused by the
grounding gate** for citing records that were not in the evidence pack. The one
link proposal it returned was **not accepted**.

> **The control is the claim.** The same corpus with `--no-llm` produces
> precision, recall, match rate and exception recall identical to six decimal
> places. *The LLM proposes; deterministic code decides* — measured by taking the
> model away and seeing that nothing moved.

## 4:10–4:35 · Scale

`ledgerloop scale`, four sizes at five seeds each:

| Orders | Records | Recall (5 seeds) | FP |
|---|---|---|---|
| 300 | 727 | 0.8971 ± 0.0980 | **0** |
| 1,000 | 2,448 | 0.8726 ± 0.0527 | **0** |
| 2,500 | 6,121 | 0.8646 ± 0.0197 | **0** |
| 5,000 | 12,233 | 0.8467 ± 0.0186 | **0** |

**Precision holds at every size on every seed.** Worth admitting on camera: this
benchmark used to run one seed and **both of its headline claims were wrong** —
it hid 17 false positives at 5,000 orders on a seed it never ran. Finding that is
what closed the last precision defect.

## 4:35–5:00 · Limitations, said out loud

Do not skip these. They are the strongest part of the submission.

- **Synthetic data only.** Nothing here has met a real bank statement.
- **Real-data validation is blocked on a truth set, not a file.** Every metric
  scores against link-level `(payment, bank_txn)` edges; real sources do not
  carry them, and producing them means reconciling by hand — the work this
  system automates.
- **Two headline verdicts are *undecided*, not passes.** See above.
- **T4 contributes zero** on this corpus, and we say so rather than loosening a
  rule to make it fire.
- **The live LLM run is one sample** and will not reproduce; every deterministic
  number will.
- **Calibration is unexercised** — one populated bin over 137 residual links, so
  ECE measures the corpus rather than the calibrator.

## Why this matters for Razorpay

Settlement reconciliation is a real, daily, expensive finance-ops loop, and the
expensive failure is not a missed match — it is a **wrong** one, because a wrong
auto-match propagates into books that someone later has to unwind. LedgerLoop is
built around that asymmetry: it resolves 91.59% of the corpus with **zero** wrong
links and ₹0 of wrongly-reconciled money, and everything it will not resolve
arrives as a typed exception with a rupee figure, an evidence chain and a
suggested action — a queue a controller can work, not a black box to trust.

## If a judge asks

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Is it really deterministic?* | `--no-llm` runs the whole system with no model and no network. Every published number was produced that way, and CI runs the suite with no keys. |
| *Could it be overfitted?* | Three-way split: the blender fits on `train`, isotonic and `tau_high` on `calibration`, every published number comes from `test`. The bundle's provenance records which seeds it saw. |
| *Where would it break first?* | Same-merchant payouts colliding inside each other's tolerance band at scale — which is exactly the defect the 5,000-order benchmark found, and the two T3 guards that closed it can only ever make the tier decline. |
| *Why no Neo4j / Chroma / Docker?* | Cut, with reasons, in `ARCHITECTURE.md` §5. The plan required Neo4j and NetworkX to produce identical decisions, which makes the database pure overhead; sentence embeddings are weak on vowel-dropped abbreviations like `RZRPAY SFTWR P L`. |
| *What would you do next?* | Acquire a real corpus **and** an independent link-level reconciliation of it. Not more synthetic models — two already disagree on construction and agree on the rule. |
